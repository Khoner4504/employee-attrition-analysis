# Employee Attrition Analysis — Bingo Boxes Inc. (Case Study)

Excel-based analysis of 12,075 anonymized employee records to identify why a fictional packaging manufacturer was losing staff. Completed for ISM 360 (Management Information Systems) at Northern Arizona University, Fall 2025. The deliverable was a written report to the company's CEO with findings and recommended organizational changes.

## Key Findings

- **Overall attrition is 24.4%** — 2,950 of 12,075 employees have left.
- **Workload is the sharpest signal.** Employees on 7+ projects left at a 100% rate; those on 3–5 projects had the lowest attrition, with a U-shaped curve on either side.
- **Pay matters.** Low-salary employees left at 31% — nearly five times the rate of high-salary employees — and account for 1,825 departures.
- **Tenure peaks at year five.** Attrition hits 57% at five years of service, versus 27% at two years.
- **Promotion is rare and protective.** Only 254 employees had been promoted in the last six months; non-promoted staff left at 25%.
- **Department gap.** Finance (28%) and Engineering (26%) lead attrition; Procurement is lowest at 10%.

## Recommendations

1. Rebalance workloads so every employee falls in the 3–5 project range.
2. Review compensation for the low-salary band.
3. Expand promotion pathways.
4. Investigate Finance and Engineering individually; study what Procurement does right.
5. Build a retention program targeted at the five-year mark.

## Method

Raw data was loaded into Excel and summarized with five pivot tables (by department, years employed, salary level, promotion status, and number of projects), each with a chart. Attrition rate was computed as employees who left ÷ total employees in each group.
